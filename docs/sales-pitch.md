# Sales pitch — on-site script for walk-up local business owners

**Who uses this:** our partner, walking up to cafe/bar/salon owners (State College area), holding a phone running the demo.
**Demo URL:** `http://<tunnel>:8000/demo/` — load it BEFORE walking up. If the tunnel is down, the page still replays the recorded session (offline fallback), so you are never on a dead screen.
**Honesty rule:** we never claim it's a survey of real humans. It's 50 agents built from the business's own real reviews. Say it plainly — it builds trust, and it's true.

---

## 1. The 30-second opener

Walk up owner working, keep it under 30 seconds, phone out:

> "Hey — you run this place? Quick one: **Ask your customers before you spend the money.** If you're deciding — late-night hours, a promo, a new menu item — right now you're guessing, or paying $500 to $2,000 for one market-research report. We built the software that reads the public record — your reviews, your prices, your competitors — builds **50 AI customers**, and makes them debate your decision with receipts. A verdict in under a minute. Give me 60 seconds on your phone and you'll see for yourself."

Then STOP talking. Hand over the phone or hold it out. The demo does the rest.

If they're mid-task, the short version: *"Ask your customers before you spend the money — one minute on my phone, and I'll show you."*

---

## 2. The demo choreography (60–90 seconds, phone in view)

The magic is that the OWNER is the one whose hands stay on the phone. Your job is narration only.

1. **Owner types ONLY their business name** — "Cafe 210 West" is the standing demo business, but the intake is literally one field. Say: *"Just your business name. No sign-up, no pasting."*
2. **Live evidence cards appear as they come in** — real scraped reviews, prices, competitor notes, each with a source. Say: *"That's the public record on your business — reviews, prices, competitors. Live right now. We pulled 150 real reviews from Cafe 210 West today."*
3. **They type THEIR decision question** — put it in their hands: *"What's the decision you're actually sitting on this week?"* (Late hours? Raise prices? A $500 promo?) If they freeze: *"Should we stay open until 2 AM on football Saturdays?"* — that's the loaded example.
4. **Debate, ≤1 minute** — 50 customer agents argue in rounds, with stance cards ("No — game day brings 40% of my late-night spend") and camps auto-forming ("Open-late skeptics") from the business's real data. Say: *"These are your actual customer types, pulled out of your own reviews, arguing with the receipts."*
5. **Verdict lands** — recommendation + confidence tally. Then STOP here. Do not scroll past the gate.

**Total owner hands-on time: two short typings.** Everything else is watching.

---

## 3. The teaser → $19 close

When the verdict lands, the phone shows the recommendation + confidence — and the rest is gated. Say this while it's on screen:

> "There's your answer. And per customer segment — *which camp you'll win and lose* — plus the top objections, plus you can chat directly with any of these customers: that's the $19 unlock. One market-research report is **$500 to $2,000** and takes two weeks. This is $19 and it took your business one minute."

Then hand the Stripe link **right after the teaser lands** — immediately, while segment impact is still a locked box on the screen. Don't wait until they're "done". The teaser moment IS the close moment, and the verdict from Cafe 210 West already landed with per-segment impact visible on the unlocked side in under a minute.

One-line backup if they hesitate on price: *"It's one report's price, divided by about fifty."*

## 4. Objection handling — the 5 predictable pushbacks

**"These are fake customers."**
> "The customers are simulated. The opinions aren't — every agent is built from the written record of your actual business: 150 real reviews we scraped today, your real prices, your real competitors. Fake people, real receipts."

**"My customers are different."**
> "Exactly — that's the whole product. We don't have a template cafe. We build your customers from what the internet says about YOUR place. Our simulator was even calibrated from Cafe 210 West's real reviews. Your segment mix comes out of your data, not a template."

**"I don't have tech."**
> "Right, you're not the one using it. That's me. You typed a business name, you typed a question. That's the entire interface."

**"Why pay for what ChatGPT gives me?"**
> "ChatGPT gives you one generic answer to one generic prompt. This is 50 agents built from YOUR real reviews, arguing your question with citations, segment by segment — plus a market-calibrated learning loop that keeps improving what it tells you: in today's real runs it improved its accuracy on scenarios it had never seen by 57% plus. A paragraph from ChatGPT can't do that, and can't tell you *which camp* you'll lose."

**"I already know my customers agree."**
> "Gut feel gets the majority right and misses the minority that actually burns you. In one real run today the debate found your customers split — families versus tourists — 7 to 0 against the move you'd have bet on. You'd have found that out with a Tuesday night that flops."

## 6. Follow-up capture (write down on the SAME card, right when they walk off)

Copy these four lines onto a notepad/card and fill them fast:

1. **Name + business + number** — full name and the business.
2. **The decision this week** — literally what they said they're deciding ("late hours", "$500 flyer run"). That's the hook for the follow-up text.
3. **Payment?** — yes / no / "sent it". Note which.
4. **Screenshot of the verdict** — snap the phone screen with their verdict number showing before it resets. It's the thing they'll reread, and the reason they answer your follow-up text.

Timing: capture **between the teaser landing and the Stripe link** if they walk off — the verdict screenshot is what you take first, the link is what lands second.
